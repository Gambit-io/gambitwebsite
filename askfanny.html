Why it matters

The questions do not keep clinic hours.

The clinic answers the phone at 8am. The question arrives at 3am. A woman rereads her radiology report on a Friday night and stops at a word nobody explained. Another misses a dose of her oral treatment on a Saturday and wonders what to do about tonight’s. The question is usually not complicated. It is just the kind nobody wants to phone about, so it waits, and it keeps her company while it does.

Their care teams answer these questions well, in clinic hours. A nurse answers one question at a time. A companion is built to answer the same question at 3am, in either language, only from documents the hospital has approved, and to say plainly when a question belongs to the care team. It does not replace the clinic. It covers the hours the clinic cannot. It never diagnoses. It never changes a treatment plan. It never speaks to prognosis. A companion between appointments, not a substitute for one.

annual patient reach of the CHUM breast programme women move through the CHUM breast programme each year. None of them has used AskFanny. Three companions are waiting on clinical review. Six more are waiting on somebody to build them, and that is the part this page is asking you to change.

How it holds

The safety layer runs in code, before the AI sees a message. A dangerous fever reading is intercepted in code and the user is directed to emergency care or their care team, without depending on model judgment. The system is closed. It answers only from hospital-approved documents, it does not browse the web, and when a question falls outside its scope it says so and points to the care team. Every turn is auditable. A clinician can see exactly which guidance produced an answer, and leave feedback on it. Clinical numbers, doses and thresholds are never reformatted between languages.

one real exchange from the prototype cleared for publication: the patient question, the companion’s answer, and the approved document that produced it, in French and English Every turn looks like this. Prototype. Not for patient use. Demonstration by appointment.

Sourcing. Maude draws on the CHUM’s own fiches santé. Joelle draws on CHUM protocols, the CANO/CAPhO CDK4/6 Clinical Resource and Jerzak et al, Curr Oncol 2025. Camille draws on the Canadian Association of Radiologists Breast Disease Referral Guideline, Journal of Breast Imaging reviews, and Quebec’s PQDCS screening programme. Individual documents trace to these sources. The product as a whole is in clinical review and has not been formally endorsed by the CHUM.

Six open

Choose the one you want built.

Each one covers a stretch of the pathway where questions come up between appointments. The questions that are too small for a call and too persistent to ignore. Sponsorship is one to one. No shared billing, no tiers, no bronze. Each companion is built on its own, so funding one does not depend on the other five selling. Companions are built in the order they are claimed, and a claimed companion comes off this page.
