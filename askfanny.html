The problem

The clinic answers the same forty questions every day.

A woman diagnosed with breast cancer leaves the appointment with more questions than she arrived with. They surface at midnight, on a Sunday, in the days between scans. She calls the clinic and waits, or she asks the open internet and gets an answer nobody at her hospital would stand behind. AskFanny is the third option.

A closed system

It can only say what the hospital has already approved.

Every answer comes from a set of documents the CHUM controls: their own patient fiches, their treatment protocols, and the national guidelines their clinicians already follow. When a question falls outside that set, AskFanny says so plainly and points to the care team. It does not browse the web, and it does not reason its way to a plausible answer.

Built for a hospital

The warmth of a nurse on the phone. The discipline of a protocol.

A companion that talks to frightened people about cancer has to be built differently from one that answers questions about a product. Four decisions carry the weight.

Safety runs before the modelEmergencies are caught in code, not left to judgment. A dangerous fever escalates in milliseconds, without ever reaching the AI.
Nothing but approved contentEvery claim traces to a named source, and a clinician can revise the wording without a developer and without a deployment.
French and English, equallyNot a translation layer. Both languages are written and reviewed, and a dose or a threshold is never reformatted between them.
Every turn is auditableA clinician can open any answer and see exactly which guidance produced it, then leave feedback that reaches the team the same day.
Never diagnoses Never changes a treatment plan Never speaks to prognosis Defers to the care team
Where sponsorship goes

The engine is built. Each companion needs its content.

Three are working today. Six more are mapped, and every one follows the same path: a clinical author writes it, a CHUM clinician approves it, and it opens to patients. A sponsor funds one companion end to end and is named alongside it.

Where it is shown

The Montreal Summit on AI and Oncology.

AskFanny is presented at Accélérons la lutte contre le cancer / Racing for a Cure, the Montreal Summit on AI and Oncology, hosted by the Groupe McPeak-Sirois. The room is the people who decide what oncology care looks like in Quebec: clinicians, researchers, hospital leadership, and the funders behind them.

Sponsors are named at the showcase and invited to attend alongside the CHUM team.
