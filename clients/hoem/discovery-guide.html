Deep dive: discovery guide

Wednesday, September 9, 2026·HOEM boardroom, 186 Jarvis St·Morning, 2 hours

The rule for the room. We are not here to automate what exists. For every step we ask three things: what happens, why it happens, and what the outcome is supposed to be. Some steps exist only because the current system forced them. Those are the ones that disappear.

1. Prospect intake and nurture

Pilar, HOEM Alex

We already know: website form creates the prospect in Rent Manager. Three-email drip sent by hand Mon / Wed / Fri. Leasing board cleaned daily. Repeat inquirers flagged as "hot leads" to the front desk. Prospect Strategy and Outreach docs came through empty.

  1. Walk us through one real prospect from first click to "new prospect" stage. Where does the data land, and who touches it first?
  2. Why is the drip sent by hand rather than triggered? Is that a Rent Manager limit or a choice?
  3. What makes a lead "hot," exactly? What happens to it once it's flagged?
  4. What questions do prospects actually ask in replies and DMs that the emails don't answer?
  5. Which channels do you believe convert, and what's that belief based on?
  6. Where do duplicates come from, and what does clean-up cost you per day?

Listen for: steps that exist to work around Rent Manager. Anything you "just know" about lead quality. How follow-up works today: this is Jarvis's first job.

2. Tours

HOEM Alex, Pilar

We already know: booked via Outlook Bookings (HOEMTours@), ambassador-led, under 30 minutes, "not a sales pitch." 24-hour reminder and post-tour follow-up templates exist. The tour-to-Rent-Manager transfer doc came through empty.

  1. Show us the handoff: tour booked in Outlook, then what has to happen in Rent Manager, and who does it?
  2. What gets lost between a great tour and a booking? Where do people go quiet?
  3. How do virtual tours differ in outcome from in-person? Who books which?
  4. What do parents ask on tours that students don't?
  5. Could a tour be booked, confirmed and followed up without a person in the loop? What would break?

Listen for: the exact moment tour intent becomes a Rent Manager record. That is a Jarvis action if the system allows it.

3. Booking and contract

HOEM Alex, Matt

We already know: book-a-room portal: info, term and room, guarantor, sign residence agreement, pay. Needs proof of student status. Next day, every processed booking is audited by hand against a checklist (duplicates, contract dates, term, unit on floor 15+). Cancellation / TYA guide came through empty.

  1. Walk one booking end to end from the student's screen, then the same booking from the back end.
  2. What are the top three errors the daily audit catches? Why can't they be prevented at entry?
  3. Which fields are people forced to fill by hand that the system could infer?
  4. Where does the process differ for returning students, short stays, and groups?
  5. What happens on a cancellation, and how often does it happen?
  6. What would you remove from this process tomorrow if the system let you?

Listen for: "we have to do it this way because Rent Manager." Every one of those is a candidate for either an integration or a Stream 2 quick win.

4. Payments and payment plans

Matt, HOEM Alex

We already know: installments run through Rotessa. Payment plans go Form → jarvis@ inbox → tracker → approval criteria → Rent Manager UDF → Rotessa → two email templates. Nearly all requests are approved. POS process doc came through empty.

  1. Roughly how many payment-plan requests a term, and how long does each take start to finish?
  2. If almost all are approved, what's the real purpose of the review step? What would you never want automated?
  3. What does the front-desk POS flow look like, and how does it reconcile back to Rent Manager?
  4. Where do students get confused about fees, installments, or the guarantor?

Listen for: which parts are judgment (keep human) versus data entry (automate). Privacy sensitivity here is high; note it for guardrails.

5. Resident life, Mosino One, and the inbox

Beth, Jonathon

We already know: Mosino One is live (Phase 1) for building access, visitor QR passes and turnstiles, with a Staff Hub. The front desk absorbs setup questions. jarvis@hoem.ca is the shared ops inbox and the contact on every template. Full-year journey docs exist for new and returning residents.

  1. What does Mosino One own today, what's on its roadmap, and what does it not do that residents keep asking for?
  2. What arrives in the jarvis@ inbox in a normal week? Who triages it and how?
  3. What are the ten questions the front desk answers most, by day and by night?
  4. How do you know a resident is at risk of not renewing? When do you find out?
  5. Where does a resident's identity live so that a message from them could be recognized (phone, email, app)?

Listen for: whether Mosino One is a platform Jarvis sits inside, beside, or replaces. This settles the "resident app vs Jarvis" question with facts instead of preference.

6. Systems and integration

Pat, plus whoever owns Rent Manager and the website

We already know: Rent Manager is the spine. Rotessa, Outlook Bookings, MS Forms, SharePoint trackers and Mosino One all hang off it, mostly by hand.

  1. Who administers Rent Manager? Do you have API access, and has anyone integrated with it before?
  2. Which of these can Rent Manager do from outside: create a prospect, change a stage, read a resident, write a note, trigger a template?
  3. Where does the website form post to? Who built the site and who can change it?
  4. Can we get read access to Google Ads, GA and the survey data to see the attribution picture?
  5. Which entity owns the data: HOEM Management, Canadian Student Communities, or the MPI group?

Listen for: the real answer on Rent Manager's API. This defines which Jarvis actions are real in the next 30 days versus which need a workaround or a phased approach.

7. Jarvis persona checkpoints

Whole room, woven in as we go

We already know: Matt wants the voice set fresh from the brand, not trained on front-desk emails. The tour guide and brand guide carry the voice well. Tone should shift from prospect to resident.

  1. In three words, how should Jarvis feel to a nervous international student? To a parent? To a second-year resident?
  2. What must Jarvis never say or promise? What always goes to a human?
  3. When a conversation gets personal (finances, wellbeing, a complaint), who is the human, and how fast?
  4. Should Jarvis ever say it's an AI? When?

Listen for: the escalation path. That is the guardrails layer of the blueprint, and it comes from you, not us.

How we close the room

We play back the three biggest things we heard. Then we commit to what comes back at the next check-in:

  • A current-state map of the process as it really runs, with the steps that only exist because of system limits marked.
  • A recommendation for where Jarvis starts and what it can actually do first, given what Rent Manager allows.
  • A short list of quick wins we could fix or automate now, separate from Jarvis.
Read this before Wednesday. If a question misses the point, or there is a workflow we should walk that is not here, tell Alex and we will fold it in. Nothing here is a test; it is the shape of the morning so nobody is caught cold.
